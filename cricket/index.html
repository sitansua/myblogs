Pratikultayah Shaktih:
Strength through Adversity

June 27, 2025 · 5 min read · by Sitansu Acharya

On June 27, 2025, I walked out to bat for Bangalore Mavericks against Maiden CC with my right thumb fractured. The physio had wrapped it. The pain was real. What happened next — 75 not out off 50 balls — is not something I planned. It's something I endured.

This piece isn't about the runs. It's about what the game asked of me that day, and what I found when I answered.

The morning before the match

I had injured the thumb two days prior in a fielding drill. An X-ray confirmed the hairline fracture. Every physio, every rational voice, said the same thing: sit this one out. Rest. There would be other matches.

But there wouldn't be this match. And my team needed a middle-order batter who wouldn't panic. So I strapped it, said nothing to the opposition, and showed up.

"Pratikultayah Shaktih" — strength born from adversity. It is not the absence of pain. It is action in spite of it.

Out in the middle

The first ball I faced was short of a length, came in sharply, and hit the splice of my bat — which rattled straight into the fractured thumb. The pain was blinding. I took a moment, rolled my shoulder, and faced the next delivery.

There's something that happens in sport when the mind decides to stop arguing with the body. The chatter quiets. You're no longer managing pain — you're simply playing cricket. Ball by ball. Shot by shot.

By the 30th ball I'd scored 42. By the 50th, I was on 75. We won the game. I can't tell you exactly how — the details are blurred in the way that intense physical experiences often are. But I remember the feeling: clarity, presence, a strange peace.

What adversity teaches

I've thought about this innings many times since. Not because of the score — 75 is a nice number but it won't make headlines — but because of what it revealed about the nature of adversity and performance.

When things are easy, we rely on technique and habit. When things are hard — when we're compromised, in pain, behind the clock, short on resources — we find something else. Call it resolve, or will, or simply stubbornness. It doesn't have a clean name, but it's the quality that separates people who show up from people who show up and deliver.

Adversity is not the enemy of performance. Handled correctly, it can be its precondition.

A note on the team

I want to say something about what the team did that day. They didn't make a fuss about my thumb. They didn't coddle me. They expected me to play, and in expecting it, they gave me permission to believe I could. That quiet confidence from those around you — it's underrated. The best teams don't over-manage. They trust.

The greatest support is often the expectation that you don't need it.

Final thought

Sanskrit has a word for this: Pratikultayah Shaktih. Strength that comes specifically from adversity — not despite it, but because of it. The resistance is the training. The obstacle is the path.

Next time you're tempted to wait for ideal conditions — perfect health, perfect timing, perfect circumstances — remember that those conditions rarely arrive. What arrives is the moment, and you either meet it or you don't.

I chose to meet it. I'd choose it again.
