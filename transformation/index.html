Through discipline, one attains everything — Transformation post 40

The Transformation — Grit, Discipline & the Body Above 40

2025 · by Sitansu Acharya

This is not a story about six-packs or Instagram aesthetics. This is a story about showing up — on days you don't want to, on days your body resists, on days when the bar feels impossibly heavy and your mind is louder than your muscles. This is a story about what happens when you keep going anyway.

शरीरमाद्यं खलु धर्मसाधनम् Sarira Adyam Khalu Dharma Sadhanam "The body is the primary instrument of all endeavour."

Where It Began

One day I returned from a 10km run — tired, exhausted, wrung out. My wife looked at me and said: "What happened to you? You seem to have lost so much weight. You're looking like a hanger."

That hit me. I looked at myself properly for the first time in a while. Stepped on the scale. Around 60 kg. She was right. With excessive running and no strength work, I hadn't just lost fat — I'd lost significant muscle. The injuries didn't help either; I'd picked up quite a few while running, even though I loved it.

Something shifted. I decided to build muscle.

It was around COVID. Gyms were shut, so I bought a few weights and started at home. It was difficult — the motivation to train alone in your living room, with no structure, no energy, is hard to sustain. When restrictions lifted, a new gym had opened 500 metres from my house. I joined. Hardly anyone was coming in those early days — COVID had scared people away — so it was just me, the equipment, and a decision to show up.

And I showed up. Every day.

The Struggle

The first few months were humbling. I didn't know what to do or how to do it properly. With some guidance from a trainer I found my footing and committed to the journey. When I started, I could barely lift a 20 kg bar. My form was rough, my strength was nowhere, and my confidence wasn't much better.

Building a body is boring work. It's unglamorous. It's the same movements, over and over, week after week, adding just a little more weight, a little more volume, a little more intent. Grit is not dramatic — it looks like Tuesday morning at 6 AM when you've slept badly and you go anyway. It looks like saying no to junk because you have training tomorrow. Small, invisible decisions made repeatedly over a long time.

But I kept going — with a stubbornness that surprised even me. I reduced my running to barely one session a week. I fixed my nutrition, cut out junk, prioritised rest and recovery. The initial results were almost invisible. Weeks passed with nothing obvious to show. I kept going anyway.

The hardest rep is always the first one you show up to do.

The Progress

Around 6–9 months in, something changed. Muscle started to appear. The gym stopped feeling like a chore and started feeling like home. I started to understand the mind-muscle connection — not just as a gym concept, but as a lived experience. I began to genuinely enjoy it.

Slowly, the numbers climbed. Eventually I was doing 100 push-ups and pulling a 150 kg deadlift. Over the next couple of years, my body transformed — and so did my mind. It was not easy building muscle past 40. Progress was slow and I was okay with that. Slow progress is still progress.

Something else happened that I didn't expect. Others in the gym started looking up to me — asking for guidance, watching my form, wanting to know what I was doing. The gym started featuring my transformation journey on their Instagram page. It felt good, I won't pretend otherwise.

But here's the honest truth: the feeling wasn't really about the recognition. It was about something much simpler and much deeper — I had set a genuinely difficult goal, and I had achieved it. That sense of having done something hard, something that required showing up hundreds of times when I didn't want to, something that took years not weeks — that's what felt good. The Instagram was just a reflection of it. The real prize was internal.

A Fit Body Builds a Fit Mind

What nobody tells you is that the body is the last thing that changes. The mind shifts first. Once you start keeping promises to yourself — once you prove you can do hard things — something fundamental shifts in how you move through the world. More disciplined. More patient. More focused.

Train the body. The mind follows. Always.

Why This Matters Even More After 40

After 40, this isn't vanity — it's necessity. Adults lose 3–8% of muscle per decade from age 30. Metabolism slows, bone density drops, hormones decline. Left unaddressed, up to 40% of muscle can be gone by your 80s. Strength training is the single most effective tool to fight all of it — for your joints, metabolism, blood sugar, and brain. It is medicine, and it is free.

What I've Learned

I continue to train. I continue to build muscle through strength training — squats, deadlifts, presses, rows. The basics, done consistently, with progressive overload. Nothing fancy. Everything earned.

I am proud of what I've done. Not because my body looks a certain way, but because I built something real — out of nothing, through discipline, on days when the easy choice was to stay home. That is something no one can take from you.

If you're above 40 and thinking about starting — start. You will not regret beginning. You will only regret waiting.

The best time to start was ten years ago. The second best time is today.
